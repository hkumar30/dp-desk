# How a day works

You are training one skill: writing a state sentence for a problem you have not memorized.

A sentence is good enough to code when it is sufficient, small enough for the constraints, and answerable from a cell. Notation can differ from the key.

Misses: infeasible, too small, vague, wrong quantity, wrong anchor. A correct sentence that still carries an extra dimension is a note, not a miss. On a problem you code, transition, base, and order get their own tag.

Before any code: name the decision, forget-test, sentence, `f(state)` with those arguments, a hand table, then a count of the states against the constraints.

The first sentence you commit is the graded one. Hints stay locked until that commit, or until the timer ends, or until you give up. Hint 3 shows an input and asks which two situations your state treats as the same. It does not name the rejected state. Hint 4 is the sentence, and taking it is a miss.

Three cards on an ordinary day. Four on a check day, all from the family you just finished. Pass is 3 of 4 with no level-4 hint. That is a rough signal. A failed check adds two days on that family.

A card is a distinct state at most twice. Later meetings are variants: a new story, the same state. After you commit, you type the answer cell and one intermediate cell. The page checks both.

Retrieval is an older problem's text, not a recitation of yesterday's sentence. You also reread one rule you wrote.

The first day of a family has no cold solve. Later days put yesterday's idea first, then today's lesson. The cap is 4 hours. A 60-minute day is retrieval, two cards, and a rule. It still closes. Two of those in a row are a slip, not a reset. Skipping a scheduled day entirely resets the consecutive count. Sunday is the off day and does not. The day rolls at 04:00 IST.

Anything that counts on the diagnostic or the posttest is an original problem. Recognizing a LeetCode problem swaps in the paired card, at most once a day, and does not count as a success.

Lesson code is Python. You submit on LeetCode in whatever language you already use.
